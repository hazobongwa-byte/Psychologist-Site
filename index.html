Meet Bongi

A steady presence in a noisy world

I am a psychologist who believes healing happens in relationship — in being met, without hurry, by someone who can hold both your strength and your ache. My practice is a quiet room for that kind of meeting.

You do not need to arrive with the right words. We will find them together. I work with adults, couples, families, and young people, in person in Johannesburg, South Africa and online across the country.

My approach is person-centred and trauma-informed, with practical tools from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy when they are useful. I will not rush you toward a version of yourself that looks good on paper. I will walk with the person you already are.

Foundations

What is psychology?

Psychology is the study of how we think, feel, relate, and behave. In a therapy room it becomes practical: we look at the stories you tell yourself, the relationships that shaped you, and the strengths you may have forgotten you have.

Wellbeing

Common experiences

Anxiety, low mood, stress, burnout, and grief are part of being human. You do not need to be in crisis to deserve care. If life feels heavier than you can comfortably carry, that is reason enough to talk.

Skills

Coping and breathing

Start smaller than you think: a glass of water, a slow exhale, a short walk. Coping skills support you between sessions. They do not replace being witnessed.

Kids Corner

A softer place for big feelings

This page is calm on purpose. Soft shapes, simple words, and no rush. Grown-ups are welcome too.

Feelings have names

Happy, sad, angry, scared, and mixed-up can all visit in one day. Naming a feeling is like turning on a light.

When worry gets loud

Try a slow balloon breath: in through the nose, out through the mouth. Then tell a safe adult what the worry said.

Building confidence

Confidence grows from small brave steps, not from being perfect. One kind thing you did already counts.

For parents

How to talk about emotions

Get low, go slow, and use simple words. Listening longer than you advise is often the most powerful thing you can do.

When extra support may help

Lasting changes in sleep, appetite, school, or play; lots of tummy aches; big fears. Trust your knowing — you do not need to wait for a crisis.

Relationships

Closer is a practice, not a personality

A few facts that surprise people

  • Feeling close is less about never fighting and more about repairing after you do.
  • Small daily turns toward each other often matter more than grand gestures.
  • Curiosity (“help me understand”) lowers threat faster than being right.

Attachment styles, simply

Secure: I can need you and still be myself.

Anxious: Closeness soothes me; distance can feel like danger.

Avoidant: Independence soothes me; too much closeness can feel like a trap.

These are patterns, not prisons. They can soften in a safe relationship — including therapy.

Gottman-informed communication

Stable couples keep a rough 5:1 ratio of warm moments to hard ones, and they watch for criticism, contempt, defensiveness, and stonewalling. The antidotes are ordinary and brave: complain without attacking character, take a slice of responsibility, and come back after a break.

When to seek couples therapy

Come when the same argument keeps returning, when you feel more like housemates than partners, after a betrayal, or simply because you want to be closer on purpose.
